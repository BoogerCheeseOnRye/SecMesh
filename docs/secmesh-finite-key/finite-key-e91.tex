\begin{abstract}
Entanglement-based key-distribution fabrics are conventionally gated by a
threshold test on the measured CHSH Bell parameter: a link is declared secure
if $|\CHSH| > 2$. That test is \emph{asymptotic}. On a finite block of
measurement outcomes the sample statistic carries irreducible statistical
error, so the threshold attaches no failure probability to its verdict, and it
requires the sample size to grow with the desired confidence --- something
embedded fabric nodes rarely have. Worse, the threshold is \emph{invisible} to
an attacker whose disturbance is confined to the sifting (aligned-basis) cell:
such an attacker leaves $|\CHSH| \approx \bell$ intact while corrupting the key
that both peers actually derive, and is accepted as \texttt{KEY}.

This paper presents a \emph{finite-key} admission gate that decides directly on
the measured block. We derive a certified secret-bit length
\begin{equation}
\bits \;=\; \keylen\!\left(\Hmin(\SLB)-h(\QBER)\right)
-\frac{\sqrt{2\keylen \ln(1/\epsS)}}{\ln 2}
-\log_2(3/\eps),
\end{equation}
where $\SLB$ is a Hoeffding \emph{lower} confidence bound on the observed CHSH
value, $\Hmin(\cdot)$ a device-independent min-entropy function
\citep{pironio2009device,acin2012device}, $h(\cdot)$ the binary entropy, and
$\QBER$ the aligned-cell error rate. The $\sqrt{\keylen}$ term is the
finite-block (asymptotic-equipartition) cost \citep{tomamichel2012finite} and
the final term pays for universal hashing by the leftover-hash lemma
\citep{leftoverhash}. A link is admitted iff $\bits \ge 1$ and both peers derive
the identical key. The $\QBER$ lane converts the previously invisible
aligned-cell attacker into one that is both detected and priced in bits.

We instantiate the gate in a complete-graph E91 fabric simulator ($N$ peers,
$\binom{N}{2}$ concurrent links) and measure it against four attacker models.
At $\eps = 10^{-6}$ a 50\,000-round honest link certifies $\bits \approx 3\,100$
bits, while a classical interception Eve, a Bell-blind aligned-cell Eve
($\QBER \approx 20\%$, $|\CHSH|\approx\bell$), and a noise-\emph{parked} Eve
tuned to sit just above the old acceptance line all certify $\bits = 0$ and are
rejected. We also report a seeded bootstrap numerical oracle as a cross-check
and document---honestly---that it is \emph{optimistic} relative to the analytic
bound and is \emph{not} by itself sufficient against the aligned attacker.
Finally we quantify a finite-size cost the old threshold hid: below
$\approx 20\,000$ rounds even a faultless block cannot certify a usable key at
$\eps = 10^{-6}$. Our security statement is conditional on the fabric's
classical outcome sampler being an honest model of a singlet source; we
quantify exactly how much of the bound survives each attacker class rather than
claiming device-agnostic guarantees.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Motivation}
Entanglement-based key distribution (E91/Ekert \citep{ekert1991} and the BBM92
two-measurement variant) powers proposed quantum-secured links between
constrained devices. Before deriving key, both peers estimate how much an
adversary can learn. The practical question is rarely ``is $|\CHSH|>2$ in the
limit'' but ``\emph{given the block I just measured, can I certify key now?}''.
A threshold answers the first question; it does not answer the second.

\subsection{The gap}
Three short arguments motivate the gate we build:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A threshold carries no probability.} $|\CHSH|$ is a statistic
    over a finite block. Its sampling error scales as $1/\sqrt{N}$, so a fixed
    threshold crosses spuriously with non-negligible probability on short
    blocks---in either direction. An admission gate is a security decision and
    must carry a stated $\eps$.

  \item \textbf{Asymptotic thresholds leak to short blocks.} The confidence a
    threshold affords decays with block size; embedded peers generating key
    under latency budgets often cannot grow $N$ arbitrarily to buy confidence.

  \item \textbf{Visibility $\ne$ key-safety.} The CHSH value only probes the
    off-diagonal (correlation) cells. An attacker who disturbs \emph{only} the
    aligned (sifting) cell leaves $|\CHSH|$ near $\bell$ while corrupting the
    derived key. Such an attacker is accepted by the threshold and rejected by
    a gate that also inspects the sifting cell.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A compact \emph{finite-key admission gate} for discrete-variable E91
    links that returns a certified secret-bit count at a stated $\eps$
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:gate}), built from a DI min-entropy bound, a Hoeffding lower
    confidence bound, and a key-cell error lane.
  \item A \emph{key-cell error lane} that makes an aligned-cell (Bell-invisible)
    attacker visible and charges her damage in bits
    (Prop.~\ref{prop:qber}).
  \item An instantiation and empirical evaluation on a complete-graph E91 fabric
    simulator (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval}) against four attacker models, including
    two the old threshold demonstrably misses.
  \item A seeded \emph{bootstrap numerical oracle} as a non-IID cross-check,
    together with an explicit characterization of when it is and is not
    decision-sufficient (Remark~\ref{rem:oracle}).
  \item Quantification of the \emph{finite-size floor}: the block size below
    which even an honest link cannot certify key at a target $\eps$
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:finitesize}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Preliminaries and threat model}
\subsection{Protocol}
We adopt the E91/BBM92 measurement structure used by the fabric: each of $N$
peers $i$ draws uniformly random settings $a_i,b_j\in\{0,1\}$ (where $0$ is the
aligned/sifting basis and $1$ the other basis) and outcomes from the singlet
(anti-correlated) joint distribution. Each unordered pair $\{i,j\}$ runs one E91
session (initiator = lower index, responder = higher); pairs run concurrently.
Rounds where $a_i=b_j=0$ are \emph{sifted} into key material; the four
correlation cells $(a_i,b_j)\in\{1,2\}\times\{1,2\}$ feed the CHSH statistic.
Peer $i$ holds half of each pairwise state and both derive the same sifted key.

\subsection{CHSH statistic}
For the four correlation cells define, as in the fabric engine, the signed
sum $\widehat{\CHSH} = \sum_c \sigma_c \overline{E_c}$ where
$\overline{E_c}$ is the mean $\pm1$ outcome over cell $c$ and
$(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3,\sigma_4) = (+1,-1,+1,+1)$ up to the standard
convention. We use the absolutized value $|\CHSH| = |\widehat{\CHSH}|$, with
$\bell \approx 2.828$ the singlet maximum and $2$ the local-hidden-state
(classical) bound.

\subsection{Attack models}
We implement four adversary classes, mirroring the fabric's threat suite:
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Honest}] No disturbance. $|\CHSH|\approx\bell$, $\QBER\approx0$.
  \item[\textbf{Classical Eve}] Measurement-and-resend on a fraction $\eta=0.4$
    of signals, visible in all cells. Drives $|\CHSH|$ well below $2$.
  \item[\textbf{Aligned-cell Eve (Bell-invisible side channel)}] Disturbs only
    the sifting cell with rate $p=0.2$, flipping the singlet anti-correlation
    flag there. \emph{Off-diagonal cells are untouched}, so $|\CHSH|$ stays near
    $\bell$ while the derived key is corrupted.
  \item[\textbf{Noise-parked Eve}] A classical Eve whose disturbance $\eta$ is
    tuned so that $|\CHSH|$ parks just above the old threshold. Because a flip
    on $B$ redefines the agreement indicator, $|\CHSH|\approx\bell(1-2\eta)$,
    and the old accept line $|\CHSH|>2$ corresponds to
    $\eta^{\ast} = (1 - 2/(\!2\bell\!))/2 \approx 0.058$. She thus hides a
    $\approx 5.8\%$ key error while sitting on the \texttt{KEY} side.
\end{description}

\section{The finite-key admission gate}\label{sec:gate}
\subsection{Certified secret length}
Let $n_{\mathrm{key}}$ be the number of sifting rounds. Following the
leftover-hash construction \citep{leftoverhash} applied to the DI-certified
single-round min-entropy, we certify
\begin{equation}
\bits \;=\; n_{\mathrm{key}}\!\left(\Hmin(\SLB)-h(\QBER)\right)
-\frac{\sqrt{2 n_{\mathrm{key}} \ln(1/\epsS)}}{\ln 2}
-\log_2(3/\eps) - 3,
\label{eq:bits}
\end{equation}
where the total $\eps$ is split into the finite-key/smoothing budget
$\epsS = \eps/3$ (used for both the Hoeffding union bound and the AEP term)
and the universal-hash budget (entering $\log_2(3/\eps)$).

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of \eqref{eq:bits}]
Given the measured block, if $\bits \ge 1$ then with probability at least
$1-\eps$ (over the block's sampling) the sifted key is uniform up to a
distance-$2^{-\Hmin(\SLB)n_{\mathrm{key}}}$ correction, and the leftover-hash
extraction yields $\bits$ bits secure against $\Eve$'s information. Hence a
peer pair that both observe $\bits \ge 1$ and derive the identical key may
safely admit the link.
\end{proposition}
The three subtracted terms each have a clear role: $\Hmin(\SLB)$ is the
per-sifting-round min-entropy certified from Bell violation (Prop.~\ref{prop:dmin});
$h(\QBER)$ is the entropy an attacker has already harvested by corrupting the
sifting cell; $\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{key}}}$ is the finite-block AEP cost
\citep{tomamichel2012finite}; and $\log_2(3/\eps)+3$ is the universal-hash /
random-seed cost. A link is admitted iff
\begin{equation}
\bits \ge 1 \quad\wedge\quad \text{(both peers derive identical key)},
\label{eq:admit}
\end{equation}
replacing the old rule $|\CHSH| > 2$.

\subsection{DI min-entropy from CHSH}
\begin{definition}[DI min-entropy function]
For $|\CHSH| = S \in [2,\bell]$, define
\[
\Hmin(S) = 1 - h(p^\ast), \qquad
p^\ast = \frac{1+\sqrt{(S/2)^2-1}}{2},
\]
with $\Hmin(S)=0$ for $S\le2$ and $\Hmin(S)=1$ for $S\ge\bell$.
\end{definition}
\begin{proposition}[Per-round entropy]
\label{prop:dmin}
A CHSH value $S$ certifies at least $\Hmin(S)$ bits of min-entropy per sifting
round \citep{pironio2009device,acin2012device}. $\Hmin$ increases monotonically
from $0$ at $S=2$ to $1$ at $S=\bell$.
\end{proposition}
Thus the honest fabric, sitting near $|\CHSH|\approx\bell$, is near the maximum
of the entropy curve, while an attacker parked near $|\CHSH| = 2.5$ is at
$\Hmin \approx 0.08$---a difference the bare threshold erases but the gate does
not.

\subsection{Hoeffding lower confidence bound}
Each cell $E_c$ is a mean of $\pm1$ over $n_c$ draws, so Hoeffding's inequality
gives $\Pr(|E_c - \mu_c| \ge t_c) \le 2e^{-2 n_c t_c^2}$. Union-budgeting a
total failure probability $\epsS$ over the four cells and taking $t_c$ so each
cell fails with probability $\le \epsS/4$:
\begin{equation}
t_c \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{\ln(8/\epsS)}{2 n_c}}, \qquad
\SLB \;=\; \max\!\Big(2,\; \widehat{\CHSH} - \sum_{c=1}^{4} t_c\Big).
\label{eq:slb}
\end{equation}
All four cells are subtracted (deliberately conservative) so $\SLB$ is a
\emph{lower} bound on the true $|\CHSH|$ at confidence $1-\epsS$; an empty or
missing cell forces $\SLB = 2$ (no certification). Using a lower---rather than
point---value of $|\CHSH|$ in \eqref{eq:bits} is what converts the asymptotic
threshold into a probabilistic stopping rule: on a short block $\widehat{\CHSH}$
can sit far above $2$ yet $\SLB \le 2$, correctly refusing to certify.

\subsection{Key-cell error lane}\label{sec:qber}
\begin{definition}[QBER]
Let
\[
\QBER \;=\; \frac{\#\{\text{sifting rounds whose } \texttt{agree}\text{ flag is set}\}}{\#\{\text{sifting rounds}\}},
\]
the fraction of sifting rounds in which the two peers' raw bits coincide,
which for a faultless singlet source is $0$ (outcomes anti-correlate).
\end{definition}
\begin{proposition}[The aligned attacker is caught]
\label{prop:qber}
For the aligned-cell Eve, $|\CHSH|$ is unchanged ($\approx\bell$) but
$\QBER \approx p > 0$, so $h(\QBER) > 0$ and $\Hmin(\SLB) - h(\QBER)$ collapses;
for the honest source $\QBER \approx 0$ and $h(\QBER)\approx 0$. Hence the gate
separates the aligned attacker from the honest link on a lane the CHSH
threshold cannot see.
\end{proposition}
The QBER lane does double duty: it is a \emph{detector} (its non-vanishing
value is evidence of sifting-cell disturbance) and a \emph{price} (its entropy
is subtracted from the certified length). A real device's intrinsic sifting-cell
error floor $\QBER_{0}$ would simply reduce $\Hmin(\SLB)$ by $h(\QBER_{0})$; the
gate is robust to a modest source-error floor but not to a floor near the
Bell-violation margin.

\subsection{Bootstrap numerical oracle (cross-check)}
Analytic bounds can be loose and assume idealized structure. We additionally run
a seeded bootstrap: resample, with replacement, the measured $\pm1$ lists of the
four correlation cells and the sifting-cell error indicators; recompute
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bits} for $R=1000$ resamples; report the pessimistic
$(1-\eps_{\mathrm{num}})$ percentile (with $\eps_{\mathrm{num}} = 10^{-2}$) as
$\bits_{\mathrm{num}}$.
\begin{remark}[Why the oracle is not the decision authority]
\label{rem:oracle}
The bootstrap resamples the \emph{observed} block. If the block already carries
an attacker's bias (e.g.\ the aligned-cell Eve's $\QBER \approx 20\%$ and its
inflated $|\CHSH|$), resampling preserves that bias rather than penalizing it,
so $\bits_{\mathrm{num}}$ can remain positive for an attacked link (we measure
$\approx 148$ bits for the aligned Eve at $N{=}50\,000$ versus the analytic
$0$). The bootstrap is therefore an \emph{optimistic, non-IID-tolerant
cross-check} (it is optimistic relative to the analytic bound even for the
honest link: $\approx 4446$ vs.\ $\approx 3149$ bits). The analytic lane of
\eqref{eq:bits}---which lowers $|\CHSH|$ and subtracts $h(\QBER)$---is the
decision authority; the oracle is reported for corroboration.
\end{remark}

\section{Implementation in the fabric}
The gate is implemented in the fabric engine's module \code{entangle-qkd.mjs}
with helpers \code{minEntropyFromChsh}, \code{chshLowerConfidence},
\code{keyQBER}, \code{finiteKeyBits}, \code{numericalOracle}, and a per-link
\code{linkCert} that returns $\{\CHSH, \QBER, \bits, \text{oracle}\}$; the mesh
verdict \eqref{eq:admit} is evaluated once per link and the honest-link set is
combined into a conference key by hashing certified link keys. The block size
default was raised from $6\,000$ to $50\,000$ rounds because honest links need
$\gtrsim 20\,000$ rounds to certify at $\eps=10^{-6}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:finitesize}).
Per-link certified bits, $\QBER$, and agreement are surfaced on each peer's
status endpoint and in the cluster's JSON output. We also had to scale the
fabric's control-link dial timeout with the round count so that a full $8$-node
mesh (28 concurrent links) no longer flakes on a small CPU.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The classical Eve is trivially rejected: $|\CHSH|$ collapses, forcing
    $\SLB = 2$ and $\Hmin = 0$.
  \item The \textbf{aligned attacker} keeps $|\CHSH|\approx\bell$ at every block
    size---the old threshold would accept her at \emph{all} $N$---yet the QBER
    lane reports $\QBER\approx 20\%$ and charges $h(\QBER)\approx 0.72$ bits/round,
    driving $\Hmin(\SLB)-h(\QBER)\le 0$ and $\bits = 0$. This is the concrete
    payoff of Sec.~\ref{sec:qber}.
  \item The \textbf{parked attacker} exploits the old rule's blind spot from the
    other side: she keeps $|\CHSH|$ just above $2$, and while $\SLB$ still
    certifies a small positive entropy, the $\approx 6\%$ sifting-cell error
    ($h(\QBER)\approx 0.34$) and the $\sqrt{\keylen}$ AEP term together push
    $\bits$ to $0$. The old threshold accepted her with a leaking key.
  \item The bootstrap oracle $\bits_{\mathrm{num}}$ (not in the table for
    brevity) tracks the analytic bound from above and stays positive for the
    aligned attacker, confirming Remark~\ref{rem:oracle}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Finite-size floor}\label{sec:finitesize}
Because the AEP term grows like $\sqrt{\keylen}$ while the entropy term grows
like $\keylen$, there is a block size below which even a faultless singlet
source cannot certify a usable key at a tight $\eps$. From Table~\ref{tab:main}
the honest link first certifies a non-trivial $\bits$ only at
$N \approx 20\,000$ and reaches $\approx 3\,100$ bits at $N = 50\,000$. This
$\sqrt{\keylen}$ cost is precisely the guarantee the old threshold concealed by
accepting a bare $|\CHSH| > 2$.

\subsection{Mesh-level result}
Running the fabric as a complete graph of $N$ peers (every pair an E91 link,
all concurrent) and deriving one conference key from the certified honest-link
keys, the 4-, 5-, 6-, 7-, and 8-node meshes all pass the gate with
$\binom{N}{2}$ admitted links and a single conference key; injecting
\code{--eve node-k} collapses every link touching node $k$ to $\bits = 0$ and
the mesh withholds the key.

\section{Security discussion}
\subsection{What the gate buys}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A stated failure probability $\eps$ attaches to every admission decision.
  \item Attacks invisible to CHSH (the aligned-cell family) are detected and
    priced via the key-cell lane.
  \item Noise-parked attackers that exploit a bare threshold are rejected.
  \item The certified length $\bits$ quantifies \emph{how much} key is safe, not
    merely whether the link is safe---a key-length-aware admission decision.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations (stated plainly)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Simulated quantum channel.} The fabric emulates the singlet
    outcome distribution; our bound is security \emph{conditional on that
    sampler being an honest model} of a singlet source. It is not a
    device-independent guarantee against arbitrary hardware and is not an
    experimental QKD result.
  \item \textbf{The oracle is optimistic} (Remark~\ref{rem:oracle}); the
    analytic lane is the decision authority. A conservative numerical oracle
    (e.g.\ a full SDP) would be the upgrade.
  \item \textbf{IID assumptions.} The analytic bound presumes IID rounds. The
    bootstrap oracle is offered as a non-IID-tolerant cross-check, but a
    fully coherent non-IID treatment is future work \citep{acín2007coherent}.
  \item \textbf{Source-error floor.} A non-zero sifting-cell error
    $\QBER_{0}$ reduces certified length by $\approx \keylen\,h(\QBER_{0})$;
    tolerable floors depend on the Bell margin.
  \item \textbf{Entropy accounting is per-round min-entropy.} Using
    $\Hmin(\SLB)$ as a per-round rate ignores within-block structure; a
    randomness-accumulation treatment could certify more from the same block.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
E91 \citep{ekert1991} and BBM92 \citep{bbm92} establish Bell-certified key
distillation; DI randomness expansion and the relation $|\CHSH|\to\Hmin$ are
\citet{pironio2009device} and \citet{acin2012device}; finite-key security with
AEP corrections and entropy accumulation with security proofs is
\citet{tomamichel2012finite,acin2012device}; universal hashing / leftover hash
is \citet{leftoverhash}; the Hoeffding step is standard concentration
\citep{hoeffding1963}; coherent and non-IID attacks motivate numerical
treatment \citep{acín2007coherent}. Numerical finite-key frameworks
\citep{numericalfinitekey} motivate our bootstrap oracle.

\section{Conclusion}
We replaced the fabric's asymptotic CHSH threshold with a finite-key admission
gate that certifies secret bits at a stated $\eps$ on the measured block. The
gate admits honest links ($\bits\approx 3{,}100$ at $50{,}000$ rounds) and
rejects---with a bit-length accounting rather than a heuristic---the classical,
Bell-invisible aligned, and noise-parked attackers, all of which certify
$\bits = 0$. We reported an honest limitation of our own numerical cross-check
and of the finite-size floor. The take-away for fabric builders is simple:
\emph{decide on the measured block, price the sifting cell, and let the block
size floor your confidence.}
